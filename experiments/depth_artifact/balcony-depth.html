No early exit is attacked more successfully than the model it exits from.

Across 18 paired comparisons of a balcony exit against its own full model, none shows the exit significantly easier to jailbreak. 7 run the other way, and those are the exits that have lost the most capability or whose attack never converged in the step budget. This page shows all three measurements side by side so the difference is visible rather than asserted.

What those exits still cost in capability

Chance-corrected retention against the full model, averaged over HellaSwag, ARC-C, ARC-E, WinoGrande and PIQA. Correcting for chance matters: raw ratios read 8 to 28 points high because three of these tasks floor at 0.25 and two at 0.50. MMLU is left out of this average on purpose. The 1B's full model scores 0.2555 on MMLU against a 0.25 floor, so chance-correcting it divides by half a point of signal and its MMLU term swings between -99% and +28% across exits with no ordering. This is what the low attack numbers at shallow exits are made of. The 1B's exit 4 holds 28% of the full model's chance-corrected accuracy and its exit 8 holds 47%. The 8B's exit 15 holds 47%. Those are exactly the arms that come out significantly harder to attack. A model that cannot write a coherent answer also cannot write a harmful one, so a low score there is not evidence of safety.

The attack budget was not equal across arms

Mean GCG cross-entropy at step 0 and step 150 on HarmBench. Every arm got the same 150 steps, but shallow exits start further from compliance, so the same budget buys less. The 8B's exit 15 ends at a loss the 1B's full model passes within the first few steps.

A fixed step budget systematically under-attacks shallower exits. At the 8B's exit 15 GCG cut more absolute loss than at full depth, 0.79 against 0.62, and still finished 1.4 nats higher because it started 1.57 nats higher. Reporting starting loss and convergence state alongside attack success, and budgeting steps adaptively, is the concrete prescription this run supports.

What this run still cannot tell you

  • There is no unattacked attack-success number. Every cell here has a GCG suffix on it. The no-attack control that exists measures teacher-forced loss, and only for the 1B, so the attack's own contribution is unquantified. With refusal rates near zero this matters: if these models answer harmful prompts when simply asked, much of the reported success is not the attack's doing.
  • All three baselines are unaligned base checkpoints. No safety-tuned Balcony checkpoint is public. Refusal runs at 0 to 4% on the 1B, 0 to 14% on the 7B and 6 to 18% on the 8B, so there is very little refusal behaviour for GCG to defeat, and this cannot be framed as a safety-degradation study.
  • Depth coverage is fixed and uneven. The 1B samples 25, 50 and 75% of depth; the 7B and 8B sample 47, 56 and 66%. Neither large model has an exit past 66%, and no exit can be added to a public checkpoint.
  • Most cells were still improving at step 150. Only the 1B's exit 12 and full model had flattened, so the matrix as a whole is under-converged and the ranking between arms is partly a ranking of how far each got.
  • TAO is not in this page. Its shards are incomplete and no finished shard yet has a matching full-model or baseline comparator.
